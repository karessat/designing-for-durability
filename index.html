The shift

Durable, repairable, modular appliances become commercially viable in the U.S. mass market within five years.

Three conditions are changing at once. Statute now compels repairability and puts durability scores at the point of sale. Enforcement now prices repair restriction as legal exposure rather than aftermarket revenue. And consumers now hold durable goods longer in every category where age is measured.

The industry's operating assumption — replacement stays cheap while repair stays impractical — no longer holds on either side. For a manufacturer, the shift relocates profit from the moment of sale to the life of the installed base.

The Bureau of Labor Statistics has priced major appliances for decades. In 2023, the index stood 1 point below its 1998 level. A quarter century without a nominal price increase.

BLS CPI, major appliances (CUUR0000SS30021), annual averages, indexed 1998 = 100. Quality-adjusted.

Over the same period, the cost of repairing household items rose 246%. Replacement stayed flat while repair tripled. Disposability was the rational purchase.

BLS CPI, repair of household items (CUUR0000SEHP04), indexed 1998 = 100.

The flat line is ending. The 2018 washing-machine tariff raised washer prices 12% — about $86 per unit — within months. Dryers, which were not tariffed, rose by the same amount. By 2023 the appliance index had returned to its 1998 level, and 2025 tariffs on Chinese and Mexican parts are raising costs again.

Flaaen, Hortaçsu & Tintelnot, American Economic Review 110(7), 2020.

Disposable design was the correct commercial answer to cheap replacement and impractical repair. Neither input to that calculation now holds.

What is driving it

Five forces, one direction

SOCIAL

Households hold durable goods longer

The behavior appears in every category where age is measured. Resale markets make longevity financially visible for vehicles and phones at the moment of purchase. Appliances have no equivalent price signal yet.

U.S. light vehicles: 12.8-year average age in 2025, a record for the eighth consecutive year. 42% of iPhone buyers retired a device held 3+ years, up from 24% five years earlier.

S&P Global Mobility, 2025 · CIRP, 2025

TECHNOLOGICAL

Capability now arrives after the sale

Firmware and subscription features turn a long-lived machine into a recurring-revenue asset instead of a forgone replacement sale. The same software layer enables repair lockout — this driver cuts both ways, and statute is beginning to decide which way it cuts.

Modular platforms, already adopted for manufacturing cost, remove the trade-off between durability and current features.
ECONOMIC

Repair economics improved without a values change

Tariffs and input costs raise replacement prices, which improves the repair calculation for a consumer who never changed values. The constraint runs the other direction: parts distribution, independent repair networks, and technician supply are thin.

U.S. appliance repair: a $7.0B industry fragmented across ~37,800 firms, none above 5% share.

IBISWorld, 2025 (commercial source)

ENVIRONMENTAL

E-waste grows five times faster than recycling

Global e-waste reached 62 million tonnes in 2022, up 82% from 2010, with 22.3% documented as collected and recycled. A washing machine lasting five years carries roughly 40% higher lifetime global-warming potential than one lasting twenty, after accounting for efficiency gains in newer models.

62 Mt generated in 2022 → 82 Mt projected by 2030.

UN Global E-waste Monitor, 2024 · Öko-Institut LCA

POLITICAL / LEGAL

Law converts repairability from choice to requirement

Right-to-repair bills have been introduced in all fifty states and eight states have enacted them. France displays a durability score beside the price. EU repair obligations applied from July 2026. Federal antitrust enforcement reached aftermarket repair restriction in 2026.

25.75% of Americans lived under an enforceable right-to-repair law on January 1, 2026.

PIRG (advocacy source; transparent method)

WEAK SIGNAL 01

Appliances are being sold as a service, at pilot scale

Bundles, in the Netherlands, installs Miele washing machines in customers' homes from €12.95 per month plus per-use pricing. Maintenance and repair are included in the fee; the machine remains Bundles' asset.

WHY THIS IS WEAKOne company, one market, unreplicated at scale. Its information: within the appliance category itself, revenue is already being collected on the life of a machine rather than on its sale — which only pays if the machine lasts.

bundles.nl, active as of 2026

WEAK SIGNAL 02

Modular, open, self-assembled hardware sustains real businesses

Framework sells laptops with user-replaceable mainboards, memory, and ports, and has extended the platform to desktops. Prusa sells 3D printers as self-assembly kits, publishes CAD files, uses no adhesive — the machine disassembles with a screwdriver — stocks parts for years, and sells upgrade kits that convert an owned printer into the current model. Prusa withdrew from full open-source hardware on its newest model, then released the CAD files under a new community license after public criticism.

WHY THIS IS WEAKNiche volumes, and the price premium is the unresolved problem. These firms prove modular durable hardware is manufacturable, sellable, and upgradeable — not that it wins a mass market.

Framework · Prusa Research, Open Community License, 2024–25

WEAK SIGNAL 03

The most closed manufacturer in consumer hardware reversed

Apple's Self Service Repair supplies parts, tools, rental toolkits, and manuals directly to consumers, and reached Canada in August 2025 — 34 countries in total. Its Genuine Parts Distributor program supplies independent shops that have no direct relationship with Apple.

WHY THIS IS WEAKOne company, one category, and it followed statute rather than leading it. Its information: when repair access became law in several markets, the most repair-hostile major manufacturer operationalized it globally rather than market by market.

Apple Newsroom, August 19, 2025

STRONG SIGNAL 04

Repair statute is now continuous on both sides of the Atlantic

Right-to-repair bills have been introduced in all fifty states; more than 33 bills were filed across 13 states in January 2026 alone. Eight states have enacted. Oregon's law was the first to ban parts pairing — software that rejects non-OEM components — and it covers appliances. EU Directive 2024/1799 obligations applied from July 31, 2026. France replaced its repairability index with a durability score, displayed beside the price: televisions January 2025, washing machines April 2025.

PIRG · The Repair Association · iFixit · Décret n° 2024-316

STRONG SIGNAL 05

Enforcement now prices repair restriction as liability

On July 8, 2026, the FTC and five states settled with Deere & Co. over restriction of its diagnostic software to authorized dealers. The proposed ten-year order requires Deere to give owners and independent shops the same tools, diagnostics, and software its dealers receive. A separate $99 million class settlement covers more than 200,000 farmers. The monopolization theory applies to any manufacturer that restricts diagnostics to authorized service — including appliance manufacturers.

FTC, July 2026 · In re Deere repair-services litigation

STRONG SIGNAL 06

Extended holding survives the return of supply

The average American light vehicle reached 12.8 years in 2025 — a record for the eighth consecutive year. Passenger cars average 14.5 years. More than 110 million vehicles, 38% of the fleet, now sit in the 6-to-14-year service window. New registrations exceeded 16 million in 2024, the first time since 2019, and the average age still rose.

STATED CAVEATPandemic supply shocks and price increases initiated this behavior; consumers were pushed before they chose. The behavior has persisted through the recovery.

S&P Global Mobility, May 2025 · DOT/BTS Table 1-26

PROVOCATION 01

What if durability is now the higher-margin design?

Groupe SEB — Tefal, Rowenta, Moulinex, Krups — holds over 90% of its electrical products repairable for fifteen years, prices spare parts below one third of an equivalent new product, and sells fixed-price repair through 6,200 trained repairers. Caterpillar rebuilds returned components with roughly 85% less material than new production and targets 25% remanufacturing revenue growth by 2030. Revenue that attaches to the installed base — parts, service, firmware, refurbishment — compounds for as long as the machine survives.

Whirlpool does not report parts and service revenue as a line item. The aftermarket P&L of its installed base is invisible — including to the market that prices the company.

PROVOCATION 02

What if the first durability claim at a mass-market price re-brands the maker?

No mass-market American manufacturer holds the durability position, and the industry's trade association has opposed repair legislation. The position is demonstrably worth money: Speed Queen tests home washers to 10,400 cycles — twenty-five years of use — and sells them at $1,300 to $1,900; Speed Queen and Miele hold the lowest first-year service rates in the only published service-call dataset. Both are niche. The mass-market slot is empty.

Whirlpool recorded an impairment on the Maytag trade name in 2024. The brand whose advertising asset since 1967 has been a repairman with nothing to do is losing carrying value inside a portfolio built for replacement cycles. The brand asset fits the durability position; the portfolio strategy does not yet use it.

PROVOCATION 03

What if sustainability stops requiring a premium?

59% of Europeans say they will pay more for repairable, sustainable products — down from 72% in 2007. When consultants measure actual willingness at the register, the figure is 17%. That gap has been the industry's justification for dismissing durability for two decades.

Durable design does not need the gap to close. A machine serviced for fifteen years wins on cost per year of service, at current repair and replacement prices, for a customer who never considered e-waste. The values case failed for twenty years. The economic case does not require the customer to hold any values at all.

PROVOCATION 04

What if the manufacturer captured the second life of its own machines?

Back Market passed $3.5 billion in refurbished-electronics sales in 2025, up 32% in a year, and reached profitability. The auto industry converted used inventory into certified pre-owned programs decades ago. Appliances have no OEM equivalent: resale is fragmented across salvage dealers and marketplace listings, and the manufacturer participates in none of it.

Every Maytag resold outside Whirlpool's channel is revenue booked by someone else against an asset Whirlpool engineered. Certified refurbishment converts the installed base into a second sales channel, a parts customer, and a data source on failure modes.

Maturity assessment

How close is this future to real?

STILE rates a monitored shift on five conditions. 1 means the condition is in place. 3 means it is absent. Research on the framework finds a shift completes only when every element approaches 1 — a single lagging element delays the whole.

Hines & McBride, University of Houston foresight program, 2026. Assessment date: August 2026.

1 — IN PLACE2 — IN PROGRESS3 — ABSENT
Mean: 2.0

The shift sits in the transition zone — past emergence, short of adoption. Consistent with a three-to-five-year horizon rather than a one-to-two-year one.

The pattern across the five elements is the finding. Legal clearance and technological capability — the two elements available to every competitor equally — lead. Infrastructure and entrepreneurial zeal — the two elements a single firm can own — lag.

Statute cannot be a competitive advantage; everyone complies with the same law. Repair infrastructure, parts logistics, and organizational commitment can be. The two lagging elements are not obstacles to the shift. They are the location of first-mover advantage in it.

What would change this assessment

  • The next UN E-waste Monitor (~2027) showing documented recycling growing faster than generation — weakens the environmental driver.
  • The BLS repair CPI flattening relative to the appliance CPI — weakens the economic driver.
  • Appliance shipment volumes falling while installed-base age rises — strengthens the extended-hold thesis in-category.
  • An appliance-specific petition in the 2027 Copyright Office triennial rulemaking — moves Legal clearance toward 1.

The position

Somebody will be the durability brand of the American kitchen and laundry room. Nobody is yet.

The regulation arrives on a published schedule. The enforcement precedent is set. The consumer behavior is measured and persistent. The niche proofs hold price premiums. What does not yet exist is a mass-market manufacturer willing to claim the position — and a brand built on an idle repairman is already in the portfolio.
