The same study found no evidence that manufacturers deliberately engineer failure. It attributed shorter lifespans to rising product complexity, price competition, and upgrade behavior. Later peer-reviewed work found lifetime declines in some appliance types and not others.

One measure of that complexity: in J.D. Power's 2025 U.S. appliance study, connected appliances recorded 87 problems per 100 units against 63 for non-connected. This is an association between connectivity and problem rates, not a demonstrated cause.

The shift · the proposition assessed at the end of this briefing

Durable, repairable, modular appliances become commercially viable in the U.S. mass market within five years.

Three conditions are changing at once. Statute compels repairability across the EU and in the eight U.S. states with laws in force, and puts a durability score at the point of sale in France. Federal enforcement has priced repair restriction as legal exposure. And in the two consumer categories where holding periods are tracked — vehicles and smartphones — those periods are lengthening.

Cheap replacement and impractical repair were the conditions that made disposable design correct. The first is weakening. The second is being dismantled by statute, though the repair infrastructure that would replace it does not yet exist at scale. For a manufacturer, the shift moves profit from the moment of sale toward the life of the installed base.

The Bureau of Labor Statistics has priced major appliances since the 1970s. In 2023 the index sat 1 point below its 1998 level — a quarter century with no net nominal increase, though it moved in both directions within that span.

BLS CPI, major appliances (CUUR0000SS30021), annual averages, indexed 1998 = 100. The series is quality-adjusted, so it tracks price per unit of measured quality rather than shelf price.

Over the same period the index for repairing household items rose 246%. Both series are indexed to a common base, so the chart shows a divergence in rates of change, not that repair now costs more than replacement in dollars. The direction of the divergence is the finding.

BLS CPI, repair of household items (CUUR0000SEHP04), indexed 1998 = 100.

The flat line has begun to move. The 2018 washing-machine tariff raised washer prices 12% — about $86 per unit — within months, and dryers, which were not tariffed, rose by an equivalent amount. The appliance index has climbed from 79.8 in 2017 to 98.9 in 2023.

Flaaen, Hortaçsu & Tintelnot, American Economic Review 110(7), 2020.

Disposable design was the correct commercial answer to cheap replacement and impractical repair. Both inputs to that calculation are now moving.

What is driving it

Five forces, one direction

SOCIAL

Households hold durable goods longer

The behavior appears in both consumer categories where holding periods are tracked. Resale markets make longevity financially visible for vehicles and phones at the moment of purchase. Appliances carry no comparable resale price signal.

U.S. light vehicles: 12.8-year average age in 2025, a record for the eighth consecutive year. 42% of U.S. iPhone buyers retired a device held three or more years, up from 24% five years earlier — though one quarter in 2024 moved the other way.

S&P Global Mobility, 2025 · CIRP, 2025

TECHNOLOGICAL

Capability now arrives after the sale

Firmware and subscription features make it possible to earn revenue from a machine after it is sold, rather than only at replacement. The same software layer enables repair lockout — this driver cuts both ways, and statute is beginning to decide which way it cuts.

Where a platform is modular, a long-lived machine can accept current components. That weakens the trade-off between durability and current features; it does not remove it.
ECONOMIC

Repair economics improved without a values change

Rising replacement prices improve the repair calculation for a consumer who never changed values. The constraint runs the other way: the U.S. repair sector is small relative to the installed base it would have to serve, and it is highly fragmented.

U.S. appliance repair: a $7.0B industry across roughly 37,800 firms, none above 5% share.

IBISWorld, 2025 (commercial source; figures differ between editions)

ENVIRONMENTAL

E-waste grows five times faster than documented recycling

Global electronic waste — all electrical equipment, not appliances alone — reached 62 million tonnes in 2022, up 82% from 2010, with 22.3% documented as collected and recycled. A washing machine lasting five years carries roughly 40% higher lifetime global-warming potential than one lasting twenty, after accounting for efficiency gains in newer models.

62 Mt generated in 2022 → 82 Mt projected by 2030.

UN Global E-waste Monitor, 2024 · Öko-Institut LCA

POLITICAL / LEGAL

Law converts repairability from choice to requirement

Right-to-repair bills have been introduced in all fifty states; eight have laws in force, with Texas following in September 2026. France displays a durability score beside the price for televisions and washing machines. EU repair obligations applied from July 2026. Federal antitrust enforcement reached aftermarket repair restriction in 2026.

25.75% of Americans lived under an enforceable right-to-repair law on January 1, 2026.

PIRG (advocacy source; transparent method)

WEAK SIGNAL 01

Appliances are being sold as a service, at pilot scale

Bundles, in the Netherlands, installs Miele washing machines in customers' homes on subscription from €12.95 per month, on a pay-per-wash model. Maintenance and repair carry no additional charge, and the machine is rented rather than sold.

WHY THIS IS WEAKOne company, one market, small scale, and no comparable U.S. program was identified. Its information: inside the appliance category, revenue is already being collected across the life of a machine rather than at its sale — an arrangement that only pays if the machine lasts.

bundles.nl, active as of 2026

WEAK SIGNAL 02

Modular, open, self-assembled hardware sustains real businesses

Framework sells laptops with user-replaceable mainboards, memory, and ports, and has extended the platform to desktops. Prusa sells 3D printers as self-assembly kits, publishes its printed-part files in editable format, stocks spare parts for years after release, and sells upgrade kits that convert an owned printer into the current model; the company states that its machines use no adhesive and come apart with a screwdriver. Prusa did not release full open-source hardware for its newest model, and later published that model's CAD files under a new community license.

WHY THIS IS WEAKNiche volumes, and neither product line sells at a mass-market price. The Prusa sequence also shows that an openness commitment is not fixed. These firms establish that modular, repairable, upgradeable hardware is manufacturable and sellable — not that it wins a mass market.

Framework · Prusa Research, Open Community License, 2024–25

WEAK SIGNAL 03

A manufacturer known for restricting repair opened parts access

Apple's Self Service Repair supplies parts, tools, rental toolkits, and manuals directly to consumers, and reached Canada in August 2025 — 34 countries in total. Its Genuine Parts Distributor program supplies independent shops that have no direct relationship with Apple.

WHY THIS IS WEAKOne company, one category, and it followed statute rather than leading it. Apple supported California's repair bill while lobbying against Oregon's, so the position is not uniform. Its information: when repair access became law in several markets, a manufacturer with a long record of restricting it built the capability once and deployed it across 34 countries rather than market by market.

Apple Newsroom, August 19, 2025

STRONG SIGNAL 04

Repair statute is now continuous on both sides of the Atlantic

Right-to-repair bills have been introduced in all fifty states; more than 33 bills were filed across 13 states in January 2026 alone. Eight have laws in force, and Texas follows in September. Oregon's law was the first to ban parts pairing — software that degrades function when non-OEM components are installed — and it covers appliances, though its penalties do not begin until 2027. EU Directive 2024/1799 obligations applied from July 31, 2026. France replaced its repairability index with a durability score, displayed beside the price: televisions January 2025, washing machines April 2025.

PIRG · The Repair Association · iFixit · Décret n° 2024-316

STRONG SIGNAL 05

Enforcement now prices repair restriction as liability

On July 8, 2026, the FTC and five states settled with Deere & Co. over restriction of its diagnostic software to authorized dealers. The proposed ten-year order requires Deere to give owners and independent shops the same tools, diagnostics, and software its dealers receive. A separate $99 million class settlement covers more than 200,000 farmers. The theory of harm is not specific to agricultural equipment: it concerns a manufacturer restricting diagnostic tools to its own service network.

FTC, July 2026 · In re Deere repair-services litigation

STRONG SIGNAL 06

Extended holding survives the return of supply

The average American light vehicle reached 12.8 years in 2025 — a record for the eighth consecutive year. Passenger cars average 14.5 years. S&P Global Mobility places more than 110 million vehicles, 38% of the fleet, in the 6-to-14-year service window. New registrations exceeded 16 million in 2024, the first time since 2019, and the average age still rose.

STATED CAVEATPandemic supply shocks and price increases initiated this behavior; consumers were pushed before they chose. The behavior has persisted through the recovery.

S&P Global Mobility, May 2025 · DOT/BTS Table 1-26

PROVOCATION 01

What if durability is now the higher-margin design?

Groupe SEB — Tefal, Rowenta, Moulinex, Krups — states that more than 90% of its electrical appliances are repairable for fifteen years, prices spare parts below one third of an equivalent new product, and sells fixed-price repair through 6,200 trained repairers. Caterpillar rebuilds returned components using roughly 85% less material than new production, and targets 25% growth in remanufacturing revenue by 2030 against a 2018 baseline. Revenue attached to the installed base — parts, service, firmware, refurbishment — accrues for as long as the machine stays in use.

Whirlpool does not disclose parts and service revenue separately in its 10-K. Whether its aftermarket is a material profit pool cannot be determined from outside the company — which also means no investor is valuing it.

PROVOCATION 02

What if the first durability claim at a mass-market price re-brands the maker?

No mass-market American manufacturer has claimed the durability position, and AHAM, the industry's trade association, has opposed repair legislation. The position carries a price premium: Speed Queen states that it tests home washers to 10,400 cycles, or twenty-five years of use, and sells them at $1,300 to $1,900. In Yale Appliance's published service-call data, Speed Queen and Miele both stayed under a 10% first-year service rate. Both brands are niche. The mass-market slot is empty.

Whirlpool recorded an impairment on the Maytag trade name in 2024. The filing does not state a cause, and impairments have several. What is not in question is the asset: a brand whose advertising character since 1967 has been a repairman with nothing to do, now carried at a reduced value, inside the portfolio best placed to claim the position.

PROVOCATION 03

What if sustainability stops requiring a premium?

59% of Europeans tell Eurobarometer they would pay more for repairable, sustainable products, down from 72% in 2007. Asked a narrower question about a green premium, 17% say yes to BCG. Both are stated intent from surveys rather than observed purchases, and the distance between the two figures is a warning about how much weight either can carry.

The durability argument does not depend on settling that question. A machine that stays in service longer is bought once rather than twice, which is an argument about cost, not values. Whether it wins at the shelf turns on purchase price and on the cost and availability of repair — none of which require the customer to hold any values at all.

PROVOCATION 04

What if the manufacturer captured the second life of its own machines?

Back Market reported more than $3.5 billion in gross merchandise value for refurbished electronics in 2025, up 32% in a year, and its first year of global EBITDA profitability. The auto industry converted used inventory into certified pre-owned programs decades ago. Appliances have no OEM equivalent at that scale: U.S. appliance resale runs through independent dealers and marketplace listings, and manufacturers capture little of it beyond their own outlet and scratch-and-dent channels.

A Maytag resold outside Whirlpool's channel earns the manufacturer nothing on a machine it engineered. A certified refurbishment program would turn the installed base into a second sales channel, a parts customer, and a source of failure-mode data.

Maturity assessment

How close is this future to real?

STILE rates a monitored shift on five conditions. 1 means the condition is in place. 3 means it is absent. Research on the framework finds that change requires every element at or near 1; where one is missing, the change takes longer to arrive.

Hines & McBride, University of Houston foresight program, 2026. Assessment date: August 2026.

1 — IN PLACE2 — IN PROGRESS3 — ABSENT
Mean: 2.0

The shift sits in the transition zone — past emergence, short of adoption. Consistent with a three-to-five-year horizon rather than a one-to-two-year one.

The pattern across the five elements is the finding. Legal clearance and technological capability lead. Infrastructure and entrepreneurial zeal lag.

Statute is a weak source of advantage, since every competitor faces the same law. Repair infrastructure, parts logistics, and organizational commitment are firm-specific and can be built ahead of rivals. On this reading the two lagging elements are less an obstacle to the shift than the place where a first mover would gain most.

What would change this assessment

  • The next UN E-waste Monitor (~2027) showing documented recycling growing faster than generation — weakens the environmental driver.
  • The BLS repair CPI flattening relative to the appliance CPI — weakens the economic driver.
  • Appliance shipment volumes falling while installed-base age rises — strengthens the extended-hold thesis in-category.
  • An appliance-specific petition in the 2027 Copyright Office triennial rulemaking — moves Legal clearance toward 1.

The position

The durability position in the American kitchen and laundry room is open. Nobody holds it.

The regulation arrives on a published schedule. The enforcement precedent is set. The consumer behavior is measured, and persistent so far. The niche proofs hold price premiums. Two things are missing: the repair infrastructure to serve a durable installed base, and a mass-market manufacturer willing to claim the position. The second is a decision. A brand built on an idle repairman is already in the portfolio.
