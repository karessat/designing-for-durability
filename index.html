The shift

The economics of durability are changing.

In recent decades, neither OEMs nor consumers have been incentivized to focus on repairable appliances, but social and macro-economic shifts may be changing that, opening up new white space opportunities for companies like Whirlpool.

For the last few decades, the cost of buying a new appliance has consistently gone down. Today's major appliance purchase sets consumers back about half what it would have in 1998, in inflation adjusted dollars.

BLS major appliances CPI, divided by all-items CPI. 1998 = 100 → 49 in June 2026.

In the meantime, the cost of appliance repairs increased by 85%. Taken together, these changes have incentivized consumers to buy new appliances where they might previously have opted for a repair.

BLS repair of household items CPI, divided by all-items CPI. 1998 = 100 → 185 in 2023.

TWO THINGS TO KNOWBLS marks appliance prices down when machines gain features. It does not do that to repair prices. So part of the gap between these two lines comes from how they are measured, not from what happened to households. And BLS has published the repair index only a handful of times since July 2024, after it cut part of its price collection, so 2023 is the last full year.

But the era of limited-functional-life appliances may be coming to an end.

Drivers of Change

A convergence of factors are creating new white space for appliance OEMs.

POLITICAL / LEGAL

Right-to-Repair laws in the United States

Consumers of equipment have pushed for the right to repair equipment they have purchased. Repair bills have now been introduced in all fifty states in the US. Oregon in particular has banned “parts pairing”, which prevents non-OEM parts from working in repaired equipment.

ENVIRONMENTAL / LEGAL

The EU’s Digital Product Passport

The EU’s new Digital Product Passport (DPP) requires appliances and other products to have a scannable digital identity card detailing repair instructions, spare parts availability, and material recycling data. Governed by the Ecodesign for Sustainable Products Regulation (ESPR), the law brings about changes in how large and small appliances are manufactured, tracked, and fixed.

TECHNOLOGICAL

Software-defined machines

With the advent of software-defined machines, some functional upgrades and maintenance can occur through software updates. The hardware is no longer necessarily the defining feature of appliances in the home.

SOCIAL

Relational retail demands consumer trust

Selling is no longer the end of the transaction. 88% of people now say trusting a brand is an important or critical reason to buy from it, level with quality at 89% and value at 88%. Trust is harder to come by than it was: 68% say business leaders deliberately mislead them. A machine the owner can have repaired is a promise that can be checked rather than asserted.

Edelman Trust Barometer 2026 special report on brands, and the 2025 barometer. Edelman is a PR firm; its method is published.

ECONOMIC

K-shaped economy

Many economies around the globe are K-shaped, meaning there is a deepening divide between the haves and have nots. A K-shaped economy increases the likelihood that lower- and middle-income consumers will buy used appliances by squeezing their purchasing power.

New York Fed, May 2026, a panel of 200,000 consumers benchmarked against Census retail data · NIQ, 2026, a commercial data firm that does not publish its sample size

ECONOMIC

Increasing input costs

The materials an appliance is built from and the people who build it have both become more expensive. Since 2019 the price of steel has risen about 65% and plastics about 32%, while average hourly pay for US manufacturing production workers is up 39%. Steel alone is roughly half the weight of a washing machine.

BLS: iron and steel PPI, plastic resins PPI, and average hourly earnings of production and nonsupervisory manufacturing employees, January 2019 to June 2026.

ECONOMIC

Tariffs are exerting upward pressure, for now

Since 23 June 2025, big household appliances count as steel products for tariff purposes. Fridge-freezers, washers, dryers, dishwashers, freezers, stoves, ranges and ovens all carry a 50% tariff on the steel inside them. A washing machine holds a lot of steel.

Proclamation 10896 derivative list, effective 23 June 2025.

Signals of Change

STRONG SIGNAL 01

People are already keeping their appliances longer

McKinsey asked more than 9,000 people across Europe, Japan and the United States who had actually bought a fridge, washer, dishwasher or cooker in the previous two years. Globally, the share who now wait ten years or more before replacing went from 35% to 39% in one year. The same buyers said they care more about a machine being durable, efficient and affordable than about it being clever or connected.

THIS IS A GLOBAL FIGUREThe survey covers Europe, Japan and the United States together. McKinsey does not publish the U.S. share on its own, so this shows a direction rather than an American number.

McKinsey Global State of White Goods Survey, fielded Q4 2024. McKinsey is a consultancy and this is what people say, not what was measured at the till.

STRONG SIGNAL 02

Large retailers have started taking products back

IKEA buys used furniture back from customers for store credit and resells it in its stores. The programme launched in the US in 2023, grew from 8,000 returned items to 14,700 in 2025, and now accepts more than 5,000 product types. Reporting describes it as still the only programme of its kind at a major furniture retailer.

Samsung does the equivalent in appliances. It applies a trade-in credit at the point of purchase and hauls the old machine away on the same day it delivers the new one.

WHAT THIS DOES AND DOES NOT SHOWThe volumes are small against what either company sells. IKEA limits the scheme to loyalty members and to furniture that still works, and Samsung does not publish what happens to the machines it collects. What it shows is that the hard part is now built: a national channel that reaches into the customer’s home and brings the old product back.

IKEA Buyback & Resell, company and press reporting, 2026 · Samsung published trade-in and haul-away terms, 2026. Both are company sources.

STRONG SIGNAL 03

The secondhand market became real money, and turned profitable

Back Market passed $3.5 billion in gross merchandise value for refurbished electronics in 2025, up 32% in a year, and reported its first year of global EBITDA profitability. The refurbished appliance market is modelled at $11.1B in 2025, growing to $37.6B by 2035.

READ THE SMALL PRINTUS growth is slower than the global figure at 8.4% a year, and more than half the market is commercial rather than household. The market sizing comes from a research vendor publishing modelled estimates, and its own report quotes two different growth rates for the same period.

Back Market company statements, 2026 · Future Market Insights. Both are commercially interested sources.

WEAK SIGNAL 01

Manufacturers are experimenting with self-buildable, self-repairable products

Framework sells laptops where you can swap the main board, the memory and the ports yourself. Prusa sells 3D printers as a kit — you build it — publishes the design files, keeps spare parts for years, and sells upgrade kits that turn the printer you own into the current model. It says nothing inside is glued; a screwdriver opens it.

Framework · Prusa Research, Open Community License, 2024–25

WEAK SIGNAL 02

Even Apple started selling repair parts

Apple, of all companies, now sells parts, tools and manuals straight to customers in 34 countries, and supplies independent repair shops that have no relationship with Apple at all.

Apple Newsroom, 19 August 2025

WEAK SIGNAL 03

DIY and repair communities are growing

Repair Cafés are free community events where volunteers fix whatever you carry in. There are now more than 2,500 of them. Between them they run about 60,000 events a year, staffed by roughly 89,000 volunteers, and people bring them more than 1.6 million broken things.

Fourth Global Repair Café Survey, Counter and Quinton, University for the Creative Arts, published 3 July 2026. 946 responses from 30 countries, a 22.4% response rate.

Applied to the business

Four what-ifs for Whirlpool

PROVOCATION 01

What if appliances designed for durability are the next white space for OEMs?

A convergence of shifts across PESTLE categories point to a potential market for home appliances that are durably designed, with modular upgrades and repairs that can be done by any qualified repair person. Software upgrades and features can possibly provide a sustained revenue source, and the certified refurbished appliance market could get a boost from the new functionality enabled by these changes.

PROVOCATION 02

What if being first to the middle of the market could re-brand the brand?

Pivoting to modular, repairable, and upgradeable appliances could position Whirlpool to capture surging consumer demand for long-lasting value while escaping the race to the bottom on cheap disposability. Being the first major OEM to champion true product longevity could secure unmatched brand loyalty, premium pricing power, and an unassailable market leadership position.

PROVOCATION 03

What if environmental and financial sustainability converge in this market?

Durability is the rare case where the two point the same way. A machine that lasts twice as long is bought once instead of twice, and emits far less over its life doing it. The customer saves money whether or not they have ever thought about waste.

And from 2030, those claims stop being marketing and become checkable: nearly every product sold in the EU will carry a passport recording what it is made of and how to fix it. A brand that can answer honestly before it is asked earns credibility that a late complier cannot buy.

PROVOCATION 04

What if selling used machines could pay OEMs like Whirlpool?

Whirlpool already refurbishes appliances, but its Certified Refurbished programme accepts only returns and open-box units — machines that never really lived in anyone's house. Meanwhile the delivery crew is already in the kitchen, installing the new machine and hauling the old one away to scrap.

Capturing that moment could turn the installed base into a second sales channel, a parts customer and a source of failure data, in a refurbished market forecast to more than triple by 2035.

Whirlpool Certified Refurbished terms, whirlpool.com · Future Market Insights: $11.1B in 2025 to $37.6B by 2035. Modelled vendor estimates; US growth is slower at 8.4% and more than half that market is commercial rather than household.

The position

Repairable, upgradeable home appliances represent a rare opportunity to enact a green ocean strategy.
