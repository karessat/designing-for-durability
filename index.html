The shift

Durability stops being a cost and starts being a product.

For twenty-five years, the incentives in this category ran against repairable, modular design. Machines got cheaper, repair got dearer, and the rational thing to build was a product that would be replaced rather than fixed.

Five forces are now converging to reverse that arithmetic: capability that arrives through software after the sale, right-to-repair statute and its first federal enforcement, circular-design law that dictates what manufacturers must build and disclose, tariffs and supply disruption that raise the price of replacement, and a consumer relationship in which trust has become the scarce input.

The consequence for a manufacturer is that profit moves from the moment of sale toward the life of the installed base — and the mid-market position that would capture it is currently unoccupied.

This proposition is scored against the STILE framework at the end of the briefing.

The old equilibrium

The Bureau of Labor Statistics has priced major appliances since the 1970s. In 2023 the index sat 1 point below its 1998 level — a quarter century with no net nominal increase, though it moved in both directions within that span.

BLS CPI, major appliances (CUUR0000SS30021), annual averages, indexed 1998 = 100. The series is quality-adjusted, so it tracks price per unit of measured quality rather than shelf price.

Over the same period the index for repairing household items rose 246%. Both series share a base, so the chart shows a divergence in rates of change rather than a comparison of dollar amounts. The direction of that divergence is the finding.

BLS CPI, repair of household items (CUUR0000SEHP04), indexed 1998 = 100.

This is why nobody built for durability. When the machine gets cheaper every year and fixing it gets dearer, designing for replacement is the correct commercial answer. Repairability was a cost with no revenue attached to it.

The first crack

The behavior moved anyway. American light vehicles reached a record 12.8-year average age in 2025, the eighth consecutive record. New registrations passed 16 million in 2024 for the first time since 2019 and the average age still rose.

STATED CAVEATPandemic supply shocks and price increases initiated this; consumers were pushed before they chose. The behavior has persisted through the recovery.

What is driving it

Five forces, one direction

Mapped to the PESTLE categories Dig uses in its horizon-scanning work.

TECHNOLOGICAL

Software-defined products

Capability now arrives after the sale. A machine can gain function through firmware for years, which makes a long-lived unit a revenue asset rather than a forgone replacement. The same software layer is what enables repair lockout, so this force cuts both ways — and statute is now deciding which way it cuts.

Complexity concentrates failure where the software is. Consumer Reports projects 31% of refrigerators with icemakers will have a problem by the end of year five, with icemakers and dispensers the two most reported faults. J.D. Power recorded 87 problems per 100 connected appliances against 63 for non-connected.

Consumer Reports member surveys, 71,534 refrigerators with icemakers bought 2015–2025 · J.D. Power 2025, n ≈ 12,755 U.S. households. Both survey-based; the J.D. Power gap is an association, not a demonstrated cause.

POLITICAL / LEGAL

Right to repair

Bills have been introduced in all fifty states. Eight have laws in force and Texas follows in September. Oregon's was the first to ban parts pairing, the practice of using software to degrade function when a non-OEM component is fitted, and it covers appliances rather than electronics alone.

25.75% of Americans lived under an enforceable right-to-repair law on January 1, 2026, reaching roughly 35% by this fall.

PIRG (advocacy source; method is transparent — it sums state populations)

ENVIRONMENTAL / LEGAL

Circular design and sustainability law

Regulation is moving from what a manufacturer must disclose to what it must build. EU ecodesign rules phase in by product category through 2030, carrying durability, repairability and recycled-content requirements plus a Digital Product Passport. France already prints a durability score beside the price.

Global electronic waste — all electrical equipment, not appliances alone — reached 62 Mt in 2022, up 82% from 2010, with 22.3% documented as collected and recycled.

UN Global E-waste Monitor, 2024 · ESPR Regulation (EU) 2024/1781 and its 2025–2030 working plan

ECONOMIC

Tariffs and geopolitical instability

Replacement is getting more expensive for reasons that have nothing to do with consumer values. The 2018 washing-machine tariff is the cleanest natural experiment in the category, and it has a peer-reviewed measurement. Input costs and trade policy have continued to move since.

The 2018 tariff raised washer prices 12%, roughly $86 per unit, within months. Dryers, which were not tariffed, rose by an equivalent amount.

Flaaen, Hortaçsu & Tintelnot, American Economic Review 110(7), 2020

SOCIAL

Relational retail and trust

The transaction is no longer the whole relationship. Buyers increasingly assess a manufacturer on what happens after purchase — parts, service, honesty about lifespan — at a moment when trust in business is low and grievance is high. A repairable product is a trust claim that can be verified rather than asserted.

Edelman's 2025 barometer found 68% distrust business leaders and the same share believe those leaders deliberately mislead them, with 61% holding moderate or high grievance. Business remains the most trusted of the four institutions measured.

Edelman Trust Barometer 2025 (PR firm; method published, fieldwork Oct–Nov 2024)

STRONG SIGNAL 01

Right to repair went from nothing to a third of the country in four years

These are 100 American households. In 2022 not one of them lived under a state right-to-repair law.

Coverage figures: PIRG, which sums the populations of states with laws in force.

The first five state electronics laws — New York, California, Minnesota, Oregon, Colorado — brought coverage to one in five Americans.

Six more laws took effect on January 1, 2026, taking coverage to 25.75%. Bills have now been introduced in all fifty states, and more than 33 were filed across 13 states in that January alone.

PIRG · The Repair Association legislative tracker

Connecticut came into force in July. Texas follows in September, taking coverage past 35%. Oregon's statute goes furthest: it bans parts pairing and covers appliances, though its penalties do not begin until 2027.

WHY THIS IS STRONGIt is not one law but a compounding legislative pattern with published effective dates. A manufacturer can read the schedule and plan against it.
STRONG SIGNAL 02

Federal enforcement priced repair restriction as liability

On July 8, 2026 the FTC and five state attorneys general settled with Deere & Co. over its restriction of the Service ADVISOR diagnostic tool to authorized dealers. The proposed ten-year order requires Deere to give owners and independent shops the same tools, diagnostics and software its dealers receive. A separate $99 million class settlement covers more than 200,000 farmers.

WHY THIS IS STRONGThe theory of harm is not specific to agricultural equipment. It concerns a manufacturer confining diagnostics to its own service network — which is a description of the appliance aftermarket as well.

FTC, July 2026 · In re Deere repair-services litigation. The order is proposed rather than final.

STRONG SIGNAL 03

Law moved from what you disclose to what you build

The EU's Ecodesign for Sustainable Products Regulation entered into force in July 2024. Its first working plan sets a category-by-category schedule: repairability rules for electronics in 2027, durability and recycled-content rules for electronics in 2029, and near-universal lifecycle data through the Digital Product Passport by 2030.

Regulation (EU) 2024/1781; first ESPR working plan adopted April 2025

France already prints the result at the point of sale. Its durability index — reliability, spare-parts availability, repair cost and guarantee, scored out of ten — replaced the repairability index and must be displayed beside the price. Televisions came into scope in January 2025, washing machines in April.

Décret n° 2024-316. A French government evaluation of the predecessor index, analyzing more than 20 million sales entries, found a statistically significant effect on online sales of more repairable products and no significant effect in physical shops. Manufacturers self-score.

The American version arrived through copyright law and a broken ice cream machine. In October 2024 the Librarian of Congress granted a DMCA exemption permitting owners and independent technicians to bypass software locks on retail-level commercial food preparation equipment — soft-serve machines, espresso machines, commercial refrigerators and ovens. Public Knowledge and iFixit had petitioned using McDonald's Taylor machines as the example; the FTC and DOJ filed jointly in support.

WHY THIS IS STRONG, AND ITS LIMITIt is the first extension of repair rights past consumer goods into commercial equipment, and antitrust regulators backed it. But it is temporary — exemptions run three years and must be renewed at the 2027 triennial — and it obliges manufacturers to do nothing.

Final rule adopted 25 October 2024, effective 28 October 2024 · FTC and DOJ joint comment, March 2024

WEAK SIGNAL 01

Appliances are being sold as a service, at pilot scale

Bundles, in the Netherlands, installs Miele washing machines in customers' homes on subscription from €12.95 per month, on a pay-per-wash model. Maintenance and repair carry no additional charge, and the machine is rented rather than sold.

WHY THIS IS WEAKOne company, one market, small scale, and no comparable U.S. program was identified. Its information: inside the appliance category itself, revenue is already being collected across the life of a machine rather than at its sale — an arrangement that only pays if the machine lasts.

bundles.nl, active as of 2026

WEAK SIGNAL 02

Modular, open, self-built hardware sustains real businesses

Framework sells laptops with user-replaceable mainboards, memory and ports, and has extended the platform to desktops. Prusa sells 3D printers as self-assembly kits — the customer builds the machine — publishes its printed-part files in editable format, stocks spare parts for years after release, and sells upgrade kits that convert an owned printer into the current model. The company states its machines use no adhesive and come apart with a screwdriver.

WHY THIS IS WEAKNiche volumes, and neither line sells at a mass-market price. Prusa also did not release full open-source hardware for its newest model, publishing that model's CAD files under a new community license only later — so the openness commitment is not fixed. These firms establish that modular, self-repairable, upgradeable hardware is manufacturable and sellable, not that it wins a mass market.

Framework · Prusa Research, Open Community License, 2024–25

WEAK SIGNAL 03

A manufacturer known for restricting repair opened parts access

Apple's Self Service Repair supplies parts, tools, rental toolkits and manuals directly to consumers, reaching Canada in August 2025 and 34 countries in total. Its Genuine Parts Distributor program supplies independent shops that hold no direct relationship with Apple.

WHY THIS IS WEAKOne company, one category, and it followed statute rather than leading it. Apple supported California's repair bill while lobbying against Oregon's, so the position is not uniform. Its information: when repair access became law in several markets, a manufacturer with a long record of restricting it built the capability once and deployed it across 34 countries rather than market by market.

Apple Newsroom, August 19, 2025

Applied to the business

Four what-ifs for Whirlpool

Each provocation states what the evidence supports and, where it does not reach, says so.

PROVOCATION 01

What if durable goods are now more profitable to manufacture than in prior decades?

Two manufacturers have already restructured around it. Groupe SEB states that more than 90% of its electrical appliances are repairable for fifteen years, prices spare parts below one third of an equivalent new product, and sells fixed-price repair through 6,200 trained repairers. Caterpillar rebuilds returned components using roughly 85% less material than new production, and targets 25% growth in remanufacturing revenue by 2030 against a 2018 baseline. Revenue attached to the installed base — parts, service, firmware, refurbishment — accrues for as long as the machine stays in use.

What would decide it for Whirlpool cannot be read from outside. The 10-K does not disclose parts and service revenue separately, so the size and margin of the aftermarket are unknown to anyone who is not inside the company. That is the first number to put on the table, and it is already in hand internally.

PROVOCATION 02

What if a first-to-mass-market opportunity could re-brand a brand?

No mass-market American manufacturer has claimed the durability position, and AHAM, the industry's trade association, has opposed repair legislation. The position carries a price premium at the top of the market: Speed Queen states it tests home washers to 10,400 cycles, or twenty-five years of use, and sells them at $1,300 to $1,900. In Yale Appliance's published service-call data, Speed Queen and Miele both stayed under a 10% first-year service rate. Both are niche brands. The mid-market slot is empty.

Whirlpool recorded an impairment on the Maytag trade name in 2024. The filing does not state a cause, and impairments have several. What is not in question is the asset: a brand whose advertising character since 1967 has been a repairman with nothing to do, now carried at a reduced value, sitting inside the portfolio best placed to claim the position.

PROVOCATION 03

What if a sustainable approach is now wildly profitable?

The honest answer is that no evidence available here establishes that, and the briefing will not assert it. What the evidence supports is narrower and still useful: durable and circular models are operable at scale, and the consumer objection that has been used to dismiss them for two decades is weaker than it looks.

59% of Europeans tell Eurobarometer they would pay more for repairable, sustainable products, down from 72% in 2007. Asked a narrower question about a green premium, 17% say yes to BCG. Both are stated intent from surveys rather than observed purchases, and a spread that wide is a warning about how much weight either figure can carry.

The durability argument does not need that question settled, because it is not a sustainability argument. A machine that stays in service longer is bought once rather than twice. That is a cost claim, and it requires the customer to hold no values at all.

PROVOCATION 04

What if capturing resell and used-goods market share could yield rewards to OEMs like Whirlpool?

Back Market reported more than $3.5 billion in gross merchandise value for refurbished electronics in 2025, up 32% in a year, and its first year of global EBITDA profitability. The auto industry converted used inventory into certified pre-owned programs decades ago. U.S. appliance resale runs through independent dealers and marketplace listings, and manufacturers capture little of it beyond their own outlet and scratch-and-dent channels.

A Maytag resold outside Whirlpool's channel earns the manufacturer nothing on a machine it engineered. A certified refurbishment program would turn the installed base into a second sales channel, a parts customer, and a source of failure-mode data — the last of which feeds directly back into designing the next machine to last.

Maturity assessment

How close is this future to real?

STILE scores a monitored shift on five conditions. Rather than calling them present or absent, this assessment places each on the Three Horizons: a condition at Horizon 1 is already part of the prevailing system, one at Horizon 3 exists only in the future state.

Hines & McBride, University of Houston foresight program, 2026. Assessment date: August 2026.

Mean 2.0 · Horizon 2

The shift sits in the transition zone — past emergence, short of adoption. That is consistent with a three-to-five-year horizon rather than a one-to-two-year one.

The pattern across the five elements is the finding. Legal clearance and technological capability sit at Horizon 1. Infrastructure and entrepreneurial zeal sit at Horizon 3.

Statute is a weak source of advantage, since every competitor faces the same law, and the engineering is available to all of them. Repair infrastructure, parts logistics and organizational commitment are firm-specific and can be built ahead of rivals. On this reading the two lagging elements are less an obstacle to the shift than the place where a first mover would gain most.

A single score is a snapshot and decays. This assessment should be re-run every six months, against the indicators below rather than against general news, so that movement is attributable to something specific.

What would weaken the thesis

  • The next UN E-waste Monitor, expected around 2027, showing documented recycling growing faster than generation.
  • The BLS repair index flattening against the appliance index — the divergence that opens this briefing closing again.
  • Right-to-repair coverage stalling below 40% of the U.S. population, or Oregon's parts-pairing penalties being deferred past 2027.

The position

The durability position in the American mid-market is open. Nobody holds it.

The regulation arrives on a published schedule. The enforcement precedent is set. The engineering is proven at niche volumes. Two things are missing: the repair infrastructure to serve a durable installed base, and a manufacturer willing to claim the position. The first is an investment. The second is a decision — and a brand built on an idle repairman is already in the portfolio.
