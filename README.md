# Designing for Durability

A scrollytelling foresight briefing on the changing economics of the U.S. home appliance industry, prepared as a case study exercise for the Dig Insights Future Strategy & Innovation team.

**Live:** https://karessat.github.io/designing-for-durability/

## Structure

1. **The machines** — an illustrative population of 100 replaced appliances (shares from UBA / Öko-Institut 2016)
2. **The shift** — the thesis and its three converging conditions
3. **The economics** — BLS CPI: major appliances vs. repair of household items, 1998–2023
4. **Drivers** — social, technological, economic, environmental, political/legal
5. **Signals** — three weak, three strong, each with a stated reason for its classification
6. **Provocations** — four "what if" questions with business implications
7. **STILE** — maturity assessment (Hines & McBride), scored August 2026
8. **Method & sources** — full citations, source-quality grading, data tables

## Data

All series are real and cited. CPI data retrieved from the BLS Public Data API (CUUR0000SS30021, CUUR0000SEHP04), indexed 1998 = 100. Vehicle age from DOT/BTS Table 1-26 and S&P Global Mobility. Right-to-repair coverage from PIRG. Full source list and caveats in the Sources section of the page.

## Build

No build step, no dependencies. Static HTML/CSS/JS. Serve the directory and open `index.html`.

Format after pudding.cool. Illustrations: woodcut-style appliance set, tinted programmatically.
